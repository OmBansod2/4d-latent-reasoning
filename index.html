Chapter 1 The Vision: Beyond Autoregressive Chain-of-Thought

In the contemporary landscape of Large Language Models, reasoning is treated almost exclusively as an autoregressive text generation problem. When frontier models like OpenAI's o1, DeepSeek-R1, or Qwen-QwQ solve a competitive math or coding problem, they emit thousands of text tokens: "Wait, let me rethink this... let $x$ be the velocity... no, that implies a contradiction...".

While this "Chain-of-Thought" (CoT) paradigm has delivered staggering empirical leaps, it suffers from three fundamental inefficiencies:

💸

The Compute Tax

Generating 3,000 reasoning tokens per prompt costs massive KV-cache memory, serial generation latency, and GPU wattage, even for straightforward logical deduction steps.

🔤

The Vocabulary Bottleneck

Human thought is non-verbal, spatial, and topological before it is linguistic. Forcing intermediate hypotheses through discrete English word tokens constrains continuous deduction into syntactic straightjackets.

🌀

Irreversible Sampling

Standard autoregressive decoding samples one token at a time. If the model commits to a flawed syntactic path early on, it must spend hundreds of tokens back-pedaling in natural language.

The Core Hypothesis: What if a language model could "think" inside a compact, continuous, multi-dimensional latent manifold before emitting a single token? What if I could distill the internal hidden representations of a frontier reasoning teacher—specifically Qwen 3.8 27B trained on deep reasoning traces—into a compact student model that loops recurrently through a 4D latent coordinate router?

💡 The Teacher Distillation Blueprint

I selected the bespokelabs/Bespoke-Stratos-17k dataset—a rigorous corpus of complex mathematical, algorithmic, and logical problems. I fed these into Qwen 3.8 27B, monitored intermediate hidden states across layers, and applied Truncated Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) to project 5,120-dimensional teacher activations into a dense, orthogonal 256-dimensional "reasoning trajectory".

The Training Corpus: Bespoke-Stratos-17k & Reasoning Extraction

A high-dimensional latent router cannot learn meaningful geometric structures from generic web scrape text like Common Crawl. To teach a student model to reason geometrically, the teacher's latent trajectories must reflect genuine, step-by-step cognitive deduction.

The Double-Edged Sword of Unified Memory

Apple Silicon's Unified Memory Architecture (UMA) is extraordinary: there are no PCI Express bus bottlenecks. The CPU can load tokenized arrays into memory, and the GPU can immediately execute Metal tensor operations on the exact same physical memory addresses without copying a single byte.

However, 24 GB is an unforgiving ceiling. Consider the mathematics:

  • A 27-billion parameter teacher in 4-bit precision occupies ~15.5 GB of RAM just for static weights.
  • A 4-billion parameter student in FP16 occupies 8.0 GB of RAM for weights.
  • Add AdamW optimizer states (2 additional FP32 buffers per trainable parameter) and dynamic computational graph allocations, and simultaneous dual-model backpropagation will instantly breach 24 GB, triggering aggressive memory compression, swap thrashing, and kernel freezes.
⚠️ The Two-Stage Offline Pipeline Solution

To survive within 24 GB, I strictly decoupled Target Extraction from Student Training. In Stage 1, the 27B teacher ran offline forward passes, computing SVD projections and serializing 15 chunks of targets (teacher_256D_targets_chunk_0.pkl through chunk_15.pkl, totaling over 30 GB across runs). In Stage 2, the teacher was completely unloaded from RAM before the student training pipeline initialized.

Chapter 4 The Mathematical Debate: Why Gradient Descent Fails & EMA Wins in VQ

Before detailing my architectural pivot, I must explore one of the most critical and non-obvious engineering decisions in this entire project: Why did I strictly avoid gradient descent for updating the Vector Quantizer codebooks, and instead implement Exponential Moving Averages (EMA)?

The Non-Differentiability of the Argmin

In a Vector Quantizer (VQ), continuous latent vectors $z_e(x) \in \mathbb{R}^d$ must be mapped to discrete codebook vectors $e_k \in \mathbb{R}^d$ from a dictionary of size $K$:

$$z_q(x) = e_k \quad \text{where} \quad k = \arg\min_{j \in \{1, \dots, K\}} \| z_e(x) - e_j \|_2^2$$
Equation 1: Hard nearest-neighbor quantization assignment

The fundamental issue is that the $\arg\min$ operation is a piecewise constant step function. Its mathematical derivative with respect to the continuous input is zero almost everywhere:

$$\frac{\partial z_q(x)}{\partial z_e(x)} = 0 \quad (\text{almost everywhere})$$
Equation 2: Vanishing gradient through discrete code assignment

While the standard Straight-Through Estimator (STE) bypasses this during the backward pass by directly copying gradients from $z_q$ back to $z_e$ (i.e., $\nabla_{z_e} \mathcal{L} \approx \nabla_{z_q} \mathcal{L}$), how do you update the codebook vectors $e_k$ themselves?

The Two Failure Modes of Gradient Descent on Codebooks

In textbook implementations (such as basic VQ-VAE), one adds a codebook loss term: $$\mathcal{L}_{\text{codebook}} = \| \text{sg}[z_e(x)] - e_k \|_2^2$$ and optimizes $e_k$ using AdamW or SGD alongside the rest of the neural network. In recurrent architectures, this leads to two fatal catastrophes:

1. Codebook Collapse (Dead Codes)

At initialization, a small subset of codebook vectors happens to be marginally closer to the data centroid. They win the $\arg\min$ competition and receive gradients. The remaining 90–95% of codebook vectors never win an assignment, receive exactly zero gradient updates, and remain frozen in space forever. The effective capacity of your $2048$-sized codebook collapses to 10 or 20 active codes.

2. Optimizer Momentum Lag & Drift

AdamW maintains first and second momentum buffers ($\beta_1 = 0.9, \beta_2 = 0.999$) for every parameter. In my model, hidden vectors circulate through 6 recurrent passes. As thoughts evolve, the optimal cluster centers shift rapidly. AdamW's momentum buffers drag codebook vectors in outdated directions based on stale historical steps, destabilizing the entire recurrent manifold.

The Solution: Exponential Moving Average (EMA) as Online K-Means

Inspired by the deep mathematical formulation in VQ-VAE-2 (Van den Oord et al.), I abandoned gradient descent for the codebook entirely. I treat codebook vector updates as an online, streaming k-means clustering process using Exponential Moving Averages.

For each code $k$ in head $h$, I maintain two non-trainable tracking buffers:

  1. $N_k^{(t)}$: The moving average count of vectors assigned to code $k$.
  2. $m_k^{(t)}$: The moving average sum of all latent vectors assigned to code $k$.
$$N_k^{(t)} = \gamma N_k^{(t-1)} + (1 - \gamma) \sum_{i=1}^B \mathbf{1}[k_i = k]$$ $$m_k^{(t)} = \gamma m_k^{(t-1)} + (1 - \gamma) \sum_{i: k_i = k} x_i$$ $$e_k^{(t)} = \frac{m_k^{(t)}}{N_k^{(t)} + \epsilon}$$
Equation 3: Vector Quantization EMA centroid update with decay rate $\gamma = 0.99$

Notice how elegant this is: no learning rates, no momentum lag, no optimizer states. Codebook vectors move directly to the true empirical center of the latent representations assigned to them.

The Secret Weapon: Active Dead Code Replacement

Even with EMA, if a code is never assigned, its count decays towards zero. To guarantee maximum entropy and eliminate dead capacity, I implemented a vectorized dead-code resurrection mechanism:

MLX PYTHON: GroupedVectorQuantizer._replace_dead_codes pure_latent_4d_mind/train_4d_mind.py 
def _replace_dead_codes(self, x_flat, encoding_indices):
    # Detect codes with assignment count < DEAD_THRESHOLD.
    # Resurrect them by sampling random active vectors from the current batch
    # plus subtle Gaussian noise to shatter symmetry.
    DEAD_THRESHOLD = 1.0
    N = x_flat.shape[0]
    
    for head_idx in range(self.num_heads):
        head_counts = self._ema_cluster_count[head_idx]  # (K,)
        dead_mask = head_counts < DEAD_THRESHOLD
        num_dead = int(mx.sum(dead_mask).item())
        
        if num_dead > 0:
            # Sample random active vectors from this batch
            rand_indices = mx.random.randint(0, N, shape=(num_dead,))
            sampled_vectors = x_flat[rand_indices, head_idx, :]
            
            # Inject Gaussian jitter to prevent duplicate codebooks
            noise = 0.01 * mx.random.normal(sampled_vectors.shape)
            replacement_vectors = sampled_vectors + noise
            
            # Re-seed the dead codebook entries & reset counters
            dead_indices = mx.where(dead_mask)[0]
            self.codebooks[head_idx, dead_indices] = replacement_vectors
            self._ema_cluster_count[head_idx, dead_indices] = 1.0
            self._ema_cluster_sum[head_idx, dead_indices] = replacement_vectors

Chapter 5 The Architectural Pivot: Injecting a Hybrid 4D Recurrent Core

Sunday morning brought the definitive realization: Never train language from scratch. Reinvent reasoning, not vocabulary.

I designed Option 2 — The Hybrid 4D Recurrent Qwen (train_hybrid_4d.py). Instead of building a scratch model, I took Qwen 3.5 4B—a pre-trained language model with exceptional English and coding fluency—froze 99.5% of its weights, and surgically grafted a 4D Latent Router into its internal layers.

Why Qwen 3.5 4B? The Hidden Linear Attention Superpower

To understand why Qwen 3.5 4B is the perfect substrate for recurrent reasoning, one must inspect its internal block composition. Unlike pure Transformer architectures (like Llama 3) where every layer is a standard quadratic Multi-Head Attention layer, Qwen 3.5 uses a Hybrid Architecture:

Layer Range Layer Architecture KV-Cache Behavior Architectural Assignment
Layers 0 – 15 Full Attention + SwiGLU FFN (Layer 15 is Full Attention) Standard $O(N^2)$ causal KV-cache Zone 1: Parse (Frozen syntax and semantic ingestion)
Layers 16 – 18 Gated Delta Networks (GDN / Linear Attention) Stateless / Constant $O(1)$ State Zone 2: Recurrent Core (The 6-Iteration Thinking Loop!)
Layers 19 – 35 Alternating Full Attention & Linear Attention Downstream causal decoding Zone 3: Unembed (Frozen generation to vocabulary)
⚡ Why Full Attention Breaks Recurrent Loops

If you try to pass an activation through a standard Transformer attention layer 6 times in a loop, the model appends new Keys and Values to its KV-cache at every iteration! Sequence length $T$ becomes $6T$, destroying rotary position embeddings (RoPE) and causing memory usage to explode.

Layers 16, 17, and 18 are pure Gated Delta Networks (linear attention). When passed cache=None, they are completely stateless. They act like a deep recurrent neural network (RNN) that can be looped $N$ times without allocating a single additional byte of KV-cache memory!

The Activation Function Zoo: Mathematical Rationale & Stability Dynamics

In standard transformer design, activation functions are often treated as interchangeable defaults (e.g., swapping GELU for SiLU). In my recurrent hybrid architecture, however, every activation function was selected with deliberate mathematical intent to prevent non-linear divergence across 6 recurrent thinking loops:

Component Activation Function Mathematical Formula Critical Architectural Rationale
4D Router Temperature-Scaled Sigmoid $$\sigma(z / \tau) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-z / \tau}}$$ Maps unbounded continuous activations $h \in \mathbb{R}^{2560}$ into the bounded 4D unit hypercube $[0, 1]^4$.

The learnable temperature $\tau$ (clamped to $[0.1, 10.0]$) governs the dispersion: at $\tau = 1.0$, it produces smooth gradients across the hypercube interior; as training matures and entropy loss is minimized, it sharpens coordinates toward the 16 hypercube vertices $\{0, 1\}^4$, creating distinct topological routing buckets.
FiLM Modulation Bounded Hyperbolic Tangent ($\tanh$) $$\gamma = 1 + 0.1 \tanh(W_\gamma c + b_\gamma)$$ $$\beta = 0.1 \tanh(W_\beta c + b_\beta)$$ Mitigating the Compounding Recurrence Hazard: In standard feed-forward networks, linear FiLM modulation $h \odot \gamma + \beta$ is applied once. But in my 6-iteration recurrent loop, an unconstrained multiplier compounds exponentially: $$h_6 \approx h_0 \cdot \prod_{i=1}^6 \gamma_i$$ If $\gamma \approx 1.4$, after 6 loops $1.4^6 \approx 7.5\times$ (activation explosion). If $\gamma \approx 0.6$, $0.6^6 \approx 0.046\times$ (signal vanishing).

By wrapping in $0.1 \tanh(\cdot)$, $\gamma$ is strictly bounded within $[0.9, 1.1]$ and $\beta \in [-0.1, 0.1]$. Each recurrent loop can only perturb representations by at most $\pm 10\%$, guaranteeing asymptotic numerical stability.
FiLMSwiGLU & FFNs SiLU (Swish) $$\text{SiLU}(x) = x \cdot \sigma(x) = \frac{x}{1 + e^{-x}}$$ Powers the SwiGLU gating mechanism: $\text{SwiGLU}(x) = (x W_{\text{gate}} \cdot \sigma(x W_{\text{gate}})) \odot (x W_{\text{up}}) W_{\text{down}}$.

Unlike ReLU, SiLU is smooth, non-monotonic, and retains non-zero gradients for slightly negative inputs ($x \in [-1, 0]$), preventing dead neurons during deep backpropagation through time.
Layer Normalization RMSNorm $$\bar{a} = \frac{a}{\sqrt{\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i=1}^d a_i^2 + \epsilon}} \odot g$$ Enforces scale invariance across layers without the computational overhead of mean-centering subtraction. On Apple Silicon Metal shaders, eliminating the mean reduction step reduces kernel dispatch latency by ~18%.

The Anatomy of the 4D Router & FiLM Modulation

Inside Zone 2, I injected my custom, trainable 4D manifold components:

  1. 4D Coordinate Router: A linear projection with temperature-scaled sigmoid that maps high-dimensional hidden states $h \in \mathbb{R}^{2560}$ into a 4-dimensional hypercube coordinate $c \in [0, 1]^4$: $$c = \sigma\left(\frac{W_r h + b_r}{\tau}\right)$$
  2. FiLM Modulation (Feature-wise Linear Modulation): Rather than just adding coordinates, FiLM scales and shifts the hidden representation adaptively: $$\gamma = 1.0 + 0.1 \cdot \tanh(W_\gamma c + b_\gamma), \quad \beta = 0.1 \cdot \tanh(W_\beta c + b_\beta)$$ $$h_{\text{modulated}} = h \odot \gamma + \beta$$
  3. Identity Initialization: I initialized $W_\gamma, b_\gamma, W_\beta, b_\beta$ to strict zeros. At step 0, $\gamma = 1.0$ and $\beta = 0.0$. This meant the hybrid model started as an exact 100% identity pass of standard, fluent Qwen 3.5 4B!

How do I prevent hidden states from exploding or degenerating during 6 passes through Zone 2? I introduced Anchor Injection:

Chapter 6 The MLX Engineering Chronicles: Kernel Panics, Lazy Graphs & Caffeinate

Writing novel architectures on consumer hardware is an intense journey through systems-level debugging. Here are three critical engineering challenges I diagnosed and conquered on Apple Silicon:

Chronicle 1: The Midnight Kernel Panic & The Underscore (`_`) Attribute Trap

SUNDAY NIGHT — 22:50 PM

"The Mac mini suddenly rebooted with a loud chime! Screen went black, Apple logo appeared, and macOS reported a Kernel Panic due to memory exhaustion. What happened? Memory was steady at 4.7 GB just minutes ago!"

Apple's MLX framework uses a Lazy Computation Graph. When you write c = a + b in MLX, no GPU kernels are executed; instead, an operation node is appended to a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG). Physical memory is only allocated and computed when mx.eval(...) is called.

In my training loop, I carefully called: mx.eval(model.parameters(), optimizer.state, loss, grads) at the end of every step to flush the graph.

The Root Cause: In Python conventions, internal class attributes start with an underscore (e.g., self._ema_cluster_sum and self._ema_cluster_count in my GroupedVectorQuantizer). By design, MLX's model.parameters() ignores private attributes starting with _!

Because _ema_cluster_sum was never evaluated by mx.eval, MLX built a hidden, un-evaluated computation graph that grew by hundreds of tensor operations every single step. Over thousands of recurrent steps across Chunk 1, this invisible graph grew to tens of thousands of nested nodes.

When mx.save_safetensors was called at the end of the chunk to serialize weights, it forced an immediate evaluation of the entire model. MLX suddenly attempted to evaluate tens of thousands of accumulated recursive operations all at once! Memory usage spiked instantaneously from 4.7 GB to over 24 GB, overwhelming the macOS virtual memory swap daemon and causing a fatal kernel panic.

✅ The Fix

I explicitly registered internal EMA buffers into the step evaluation: mx.eval(model.trainable_parameters(), optimizer.state, [vq._ema_cluster_sum for vq in model.vqs], [vq._ema_cluster_count for vq in model.vqs]). Instantly, the memory graph was pruned at every step, and memory usage remained permanently anchored at 4.70 GB with zero leaks.

Chronicle 2: The In-Loop Conversion Tax

Early iterations of my training loop ran at a sluggish 1.35 seconds per step. Profiling revealed that inside the forward pass, diagnostic logging was calling .item() and np.array() to compute loss components on active MLX arrays.

Calling .item() forces a synchronous GPU-to-CPU roundtrip: the CPU halts, waits for the Metal GPU shader to finish execution, retrieves the scalar, and only then continues.

The Solution: I decoupled all scalar evaluations from the forward pass, moving metric printing strictly inside a if step % 25 == 0: block. Training throughput leaped from 1.35s to 0.75s per step—a 44% acceleration!

Chronicle 3: The 45-Minute Mystery & `caffeinate`

During extended background training while I was away from the keyboard, the process mysteriously paused every 45 minutes.

I discovered that macOS Energy Saver settings automatically put idle systems to sleep after 45 minutes of keyboard/mouse inactivity, suspending terminal background daemons.

The fix was the classic Unix command built into macOS:
caffeinate -dis python train_hybrid_4d.py --start-chunk 10
The flags -d (prevent display sleep), -i (prevent idle sleep), and -s (prevent system sleep) kept the M4 Pro awake and computing relentlessly.

In CUDA on NVIDIA hardware, looking up an out-of-bounds embedding token (e.g., index $248068$ when the table only has $151936$ rows) triggers a device-side assert: CUDA error: device-side assert triggered.

On Apple Silicon with MLX Metal shaders, memory access bounds violations inside Metal Shading Language kernels trigger an immediate driver-level process abort. Because the termination is executed by the Metal driver rather than the Python interpreter, Python never catches an exception and exits cleanly with code 0!

This revealed a profound theoretical lesson for the entire field of knowledge distillation:

🚨 The Fundamental Law of Latent Distillation

You cannot distill latent layer trajectories across mismatched tokenizers.

Even if you clamp token IDs to avoid out-of-bounds crashes, token index $t$ in a 248k vocabulary represents a completely different lexical boundary than token index $t$ in a 151k vocabulary. Word merges, subword pieces, and punctuation split points do not align. Forcing a student to match teacher latent activations at misaligned token timestamps destroys cross-attention coherence.

Chapter 8 Multi-Task Distillation Loss & Empirical Convergence Graphs

The mathematical objective powering the Hybrid 4D Recurrent model balances four complementary loss terms:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}} + 5.0 \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{Trajectory}} + 1.0 \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{VQ}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{Router Entropy}}$$
Equation 4: The 4-Part Multi-Task Loss Formulation
  1. Cross-Entropy Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}$): Standard next-token prediction loss ensuring the student preserves its natural language generation capability.
  2. Trajectory Distillation Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{Trajectory}}$): A combined Mean Squared Error (MSE) and Cosine Distance against the teacher's SVD 256D representations: $$\mathcal{L}_{\text{Trajectory}} = \| h_{256} - y_{\text{teacher}} \|_2^2 + \left(1 - \frac{h_{256} \cdot y_{\text{teacher}}}{\|h_{256}\|_2 \|y_{\text{teacher}}\|_2}\right)$$
  3. VQ Commitment Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{VQ}}$): Standard commitment penalty encouraging encoder representations to commit to codebook vectors without drifting: $$\mathcal{L}_{\text{VQ}} = \| \text{sg}[z_q] - h_{\text{mod}} \|_2^2 + 0.25 \cdot \| z_q - \text{sg}[h_{\text{mod}}] \|_2^2$$
  4. Router Entropy Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{Router Entropy}}$): Maximizing the entropy of the 4D coordinate assignments to prevent the router from collapsing into a single static corner of the hypercube.

Chapter 10 Hard-Won Lessons & The Frontier Ahead

When working at the intersection of consumer hardware constraints and novel neural architectures, failure is not the opposite of progress—it is the raw data that informs real breakthroughs.

1. Distillation Demands Lexical Parity

Never attempt intermediate representation distillation across different tokenizers. Even small differences in subword vocabularies completely scramble intermediate trajectory time-steps.

2. EMA Is Essential for Discrete Codebooks

Gradient descent on vector quantization codebooks is fundamentally broken in recurrent settings. Exponential Moving Average centroid updates with active dead-code resurrection guarantee 100% representational capacity.

3. Exploit Hybrid Linear Attention

Modern models like Qwen 3.5 contain hidden linear attention blocks (GDN). These stateless layers can be looped recurrently for System 2 thinking without incurring any KV-cache memory explosion.

4. Master the Lazy Graph Compiler

When working with Apple MLX, understand that lazy computation graphs will silently accumulate in unified memory if non-public attributes bypass mx.eval. Explicit evaluation is the difference between a smooth training run and a macOS kernel panic.
